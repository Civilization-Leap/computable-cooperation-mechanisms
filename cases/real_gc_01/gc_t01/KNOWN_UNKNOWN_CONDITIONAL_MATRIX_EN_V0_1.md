## Known reports: what the cited source says, not independent validation

| ID / question | Reported finding | Scope / what it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K01 / Border incidence | USITC estimates near-full pass-through to U.S. importers for Section 232/301-affected imports, 2018–21. [S1] | Not a constant across products/years; importer incidence is not identical to consumer incidence. |
| K02 / Direct sectors | Section 301 estimates: imports from China −13%; U.S. production value +0.4%; U.S. product prices +0.2%. [S1] | Not additive welfare components. The report omits full Section 301 downstream, economy-wide, long-run and security effects. |
| K03 / Jobs and feedback | Flaaen/Pierce distinguish protection, input costs and retaliation; more-exposed manufacturing industries show relative employment declines in 2018–19. [S2] | Industry-relative effects, not losses for every protected firm/worker or a 2026 forecast. |
| K04 / Early prices | By March 2025, estimated February–March China-tariff effects were +0.3% on core-goods PCE prices and +0.1% on core PCE. [S3] | Early price-level effects, not annual inflation increases; the two effects cannot be added. |
| K05 / Substitution lag | The 2024 Fed study finds sourcing-substitution elasticity away from China strengthened noticeably after roughly 3–4 years. [S4] | Not each firm's switching deadline; does not establish equal exit and re-entry costs. |
| K06 / Third parties | A Fed decomposition attributes about 53% of Mexico's U.S.-export gains to identified trade diversion. [S5] | The denominator is export gains, not GDP or welfare; wages, ownership, environment and net benefit remain separate. |
| K07 / 2025 study | The March BPEA draft estimates approximately 90% importer-price pass-through and reports conditional short-run model results below. [S6] | Covers overall U.S. 2025 tariffs and modeled retaliation, not just bilateral measures; not a CCM measurement. |

Research estimates retain `THIRD_PARTY_ESTIMATE`; source provenance is separately `OFFICIAL_SOURCE` or `THIRD_PARTY_SOURCE`. Fed authors' research is not an institutional policy position.

## Conditional author-model results: not a confidence interval

| ID | Assumptions in the specified draft | Reported welfare change / location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C01 | Labor immobile across sectors; without terms-of-trade adjustment | −0.13% GDP / Table 6 [S6] |
| C02 | Labor immobile across sectors; with terms-of-trade adjustment | +0.10% GDP / Table 6 [S6] |
| C03 | Labor mobile across sectors; without terms-of-trade adjustment | −0.50% GDP / Table A.8 [S6] |
| C04 | Labor mobile across sectors; with terms-of-trade adjustment | +0.28% GDP / Table A.8 [S6] |

These are counterfactual model scenarios, not realized values, confidence bounds or probabilities. Keep the two labor settings separate. Table 6's channel columns are **not an additive decomposition**. Terms-of-trade adjustment concerns import and U.S. producer/export prices, not foreign price cuts alone. The draft's tariff-revenue components are model counterfactuals, not annual fiscal receipts. The source matrix used the accessible March draft; it did not verify equivalence to later NBER versions. CCM's endpoint classifier does not replicate the structural model.
