# Seed Network Map — first-wave dissemination

This is a small targeting map, not a mass-outreach list. The project should prefer a few high-relevance nodes over broad unsolicited distribution.

## A. Research / collective intelligence

1. **MIT Center for Collective Intelligence** — relevant because its work includes collective intelligence, human–AI teams, supermind design, and online deliberation/agent-based negotiation.
2. **ETH Zurich Computational Social Science (COSS)** — relevant because its research explicitly covers social coordination, cooperation, norms, conflict, evolutionary game theory, and agent-based simulation.
3. **UMass Computational Social Science Institute (CSSI)** — relevant as an interdisciplinary computational-social-science community spanning social science, statistics, and computer science.
4. **Collective Intelligence Project (CIP)** — relevant because it develops open approaches for collective input, AI governance, and community-directed systems.

## B. Civic / public-interest technology

5. **Citizens Foundation** — relevant as an open-source collective-intelligence and citizen-engagement organization using AI in public decision processes.
6. **Open Civic Tech** — relevant because it organizes open-source public-interest technology contributors and cross-sector civic-tech collaboration.
7. **Civic Tech Field Guide** — relevant as a discovery directory and field map for civic-tech projects, research, communities, and working groups.
8. **W3C Civic Technology Community Group** — relevant as a public community focused on civic technology, open government, AI, and Web-based civic applications.

## C. Developer discovery

9. **GitHub topic discovery** — prioritize established topics that match the actual project: `computational-social-science`, `multi-agent-systems`, `mechanism-design` (after confirming repository-topic availability in the GitHub UI).
10. **Hacker News / Show HN** — use the short developer post in `OUTREACH_KIT.md`; invite counterexamples and independent implementations rather than endorsements.

## Outreach rule

Do not contact all nodes repeatedly. One concise first-wave message per selected node is enough. Stop active outreach after the first wave and evaluate only substantive signals: external issues/discussions, forks, independent implementations, citations, domain examples, or serious methodological feedback.

## Suggested first-wave order

1. GitHub-native discovery and contribution challenges (already live).
2. One developer-facing post (Show HN or equivalent).
3. 5–8 individually selected research/civic nodes from the list above.
4. Wait and observe before any second wave.

The initiator is not committing to operate a community, provide consulting, or answer every downstream request.
